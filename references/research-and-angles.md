# Research & Angles

The two things that make this campaign work: a deep enough understanding of the
company to write about it many ways, and a discipline of assigning every platform a
genuinely different angle so the articles don't cannibalise each other in search.

## The positioning brief

After reading the company's site (homepage, pricing, features, about, blog, any
"for professionals/teams" page), write a brief with these sections. Save it to a
file — every article draws from it, so it's the single source of truth.

```
# <Company> — Positioning Brief

## What it does
One or two plain sentences. What problem, for whom, how.

## Core features
Bullet the real features. Mark the ONE that is the genuine technical moat —
the thing competitors can't trivially copy. This becomes the spine of the
technical/founder angles.

## Customer segments
Usually two or more (e.g. consumers AND professionals). Each is a different
audience you can write a whole article for. Note what each one cares about.

## Business model & pricing
Price points, subscription vs one-off, and WHY the pricing is shaped that way —
the "why" is often a story in itself.

## Competitors & the category failure mode
Who else is in the space, and the common way products in this category fail or
disappoint. The failure mode is rhetorical gold — most strong angles contrast
the company against it.

## Selling points (most concrete first)
Numbers, speed, guarantees, price, outputs. Concrete claims are what make an
article persuasive rather than fluffy.

## Brand voice & self-beliefs
How they talk, and what they clearly believe about their space. Match this when
you want the article to sound like the company; deliberately break from it when
an angle calls for an outside/skeptical voice.

## Backlink targets (KEEP CONSISTENT across all articles)
- <homepage>
- <deep page 1 — e.g. comparison/listicle post>
- <deep page 2 — e.g. "for professionals" page>
- <deep page 3 — e.g. features page>

## Copy rules that bind every syndicated article
Anything the company's own site does NOT claim, and articles must not claim
either — e.g. a white-label supplier/vendor that must never be named, a tier
that's built but not yet purchasable and must not be implied as already bought,
a competitor that can be named in a comparison but never implied as a partner
or integration. Pull these from the site's own hedges and disclaimers; if the
homepage is careful not to say something, the articles have to be equally
careful.

## Platform → angle log
Fill this in as you go — one row per platform, added the moment that platform's
article goes live, not batched at the end. This is the actual record of what
ran where; keep it current or it stops being trustworthy.

| Platform | Angle | Voice | Links | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

## The duplicate-content rule (why angles matter)

Search engines penalise near-identical content: publish the same article twenty
times and Google keeps one as canonical and discounts the rest. So the goal is
twenty articles that share **backlinks and core facts** but almost nothing else —
different frame, audience, structure, opening, and voice. If two drafts could
swap headlines without anyone noticing, they're too similar; re-angle one.

## What "an angle" is

An angle = **frame × audience × format × voice**. Vary all four across platforms:

- **Frame**: the argument or story spine — a comparison, a founder lesson, a
  personal cost-saving story, a trend piece, a technical thesis, a myth-buster, a
  how-to, a "state of the category in <year>".
- **Audience**: which customer segment (or an outside observer — builders,
  investors, skeptics) it speaks to.
- **Format**: listicle, narrative essay, Q&A, teardown, PSA, case study, guide.
- **Voice**: company-brand voice, neutral journalist, enthusiastic first-person
  user, contrarian founder, dry analyst.

## Angle bank

Reusable frames — pick a different one per platform and fill it with the brief's
facts:

1. **Listicle / comparison** — "The N best <category> tools in <year>" with the
   company positioned by its differentiator. Great for Medium; broad and search-y.
2. **Founder retrospective** — "The moat wasn't the model: what building <company>
   taught me about <X>." First person, lessons-for-builders. Ideal for
   IndieHackers.
3. **Technical thesis** — "Why <naive approach> dies and <company's approach>
   wins," using the company as the worked example. Ideal for HackerNoon/Dev.to.
   Add an affiliation disclosure line.
4. **Personal cost-saving story** — "I almost paid $X for <thing>. Here's what I did
   instead." Warm, narrative, first-person. Great for personal blogs (Mataroa,
   Bear Blog, Write.as).
5. **Trend / state-of-the-category** — "Why <year> is the year <category> finally
   works." Analytical, forward-looking.
6. **Breezy PSA** — casual, excited, social-native. "you can do <thing> now and it's
   weirdly cheap." Great for Tumblr.
7. **Professional/B2B angle** — speak to the pro segment: how it changes their
   workflow, sales cycle, margins. Links to the "for professionals" page.
8. **Myth-buster** — "No, <common belief about category> isn't true anymore."
9. **How-to / walkthrough** — "How to <achieve outcome> in <time> with <company>."
10. **Q&A / answer** — direct, useful answer to a real question. Best for Quora.

## Worked platform → angle mapping

A pattern that has worked (adapt to the company). Notice the dofollow platforms get
strong, keyword-relevant angles because their links carry the most SEO weight.

| Platform | Angle | Voice | Links |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | Listicle / comparison | neutral | nofollow |
| Dev.to | Technical thesis | builder | nofollow |
| HackerNoon | Technical thesis (deeper) + disclosure | analyst | nofollow |
| IndieHackers | Founder retrospective | first-person founder | nofollow |
| Bear Blog | Personal cost-saving story | first-person user | **dofollow** |
| Mataroa | Personal cost-saving story (variant) | first-person user | **dofollow** |
| Blogger | Trend / state-of-the-category | brand | **dofollow** |
| Write.as | State-of-the-category (variant) | neutral | nofollow |
| Tumblr | Breezy PSA | excited first-person | nofollow |
| Quora | Q&A / answer | helpful | nofollow |
| Telegraph | How-to / walkthrough | neutral | nofollow |
| Rentry / Pastebin | Plain summary / quick reference | neutral | nofollow |
| Hashnode | Professional/B2B angle | brand | nofollow |
| Notion / Google Docs / HackMD | Long-form guide (indexable once public) | brand | indexable |

Keep the same 3–4 backlink URLs in every article; only the surrounding article
changes. That consistency is what builds the link profile while the angle variety is
what keeps each page individually rankable.

## Disclosure

On platforms with an editorial/attestation step (HackerNoon) or a strong
community-honesty norm (Quora, IndieHackers, Dev.to), include a short, honest
disclosure of the affiliation with the company, e.g. "Disclosure: I'm affiliated
with <company>, which I use here as the worked example." It's both the right thing
and keeps the posts from being pulled as undisclosed promotion.
